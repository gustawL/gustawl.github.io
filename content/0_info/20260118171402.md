I’m publishing a roadmap for this project as part of my practice (human-led, AI-assisted).

You might wonder why I’m listing my ideas publicly, or ask the critical question: “Is that wise?” Personally, I don’t worry about competition. Nor do I fear that someone will “steal my ideas” and build/make them faster.

I’m not afraid because I know that everyone (including me and you) has their own unique style, character… a mark. Knowing that, I don’t worry much about it.

I am one of a kind - just like everyone else. "==No one is perfect, and that’s perfect==". [^1]

> [!tip] When you don't know what to do, pick and do whatever from the list. 

> [!tip] The literature list is now ordered from highest to lowest priority (1 = highest).

> [!note] Every record here have purpose, or destination.

> [!warning] Don't worry. No rush. You don't need to publish everyday (but do something in that direction - learning, online studies, reading, practicing critical thinking on ai) ... learn your craft.

### Literature (other)
Books below are copyrighted, no easy way here. 
- I’m currently reading "_Reading Like a Writer_". This book is often recommended as “to read before any other book”. With it, I can build a solid foundations, discover interesting authors, and verify sources/statements for practice - there are a lot of possibilities with just that one book. I’m on the chapter about paragraphs. I don't think I will finish it before studies. . . Maybe I should focus only on (necessary to survive) skill-based knowledge. Selection must be done; I waste too much time digging into every single trope.
- _Letters from Cythera_, Jaz Coleman's musical legacy inspired many great artists (like Kurt Cobain), and I feel that he has valuable knowledge to share. One of his greatest hits, "Eighties", is timeless. It survived because it touches on the universal human experience, not just the realities of a single decade. I'm living in the '20s, and it feels strikingly similar. I think the same cognitive and social patterns keep returning, while only their implementation changes—tools, scale, and speed. <br><br>
	>[!note] Note to self
	> Check "Elective Affinities" by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe. This concept is close enough to what i have on mind and looking for.
