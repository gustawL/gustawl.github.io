Back in the day, people spent days tweaking browser privacy settings in `about:config`. Later, we got `user.js` templates... i never copied them blindly, but those templates **were a godsend**: you could pick a solid baseline and then adjust it to your needs — a huge time saver. Similarly today many privacy-focused browsers come with their own profiles and default configurations that are worth revisiting and tuning according our preferences.
## Useful when…

- **You don’t want advertisers following you around**:  
    Many sites try to track what you read, watch, or buy. Privacy browsers block a lot of that tracking. Ads were, and always will be annoying.
