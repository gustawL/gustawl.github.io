The blog exists and works the way I wanted… at least halfway. Honestly, there’s still a lot of work to do.

The website meets my expectations and does what it’s meant to do. I got my first emails, and for a moment I felt good again (maybe even too good) and a bit more confident. The blog is reachable — it shows up in Google… Bing may still be catching up… I don’t know if it’s a skill issue, or just a normal step on the new journey of "the dark arts of SEO".

In that moment of happiness, I didn’t care whether spam had hit my inbox, or whether it was a fake offer.

I was simply happy that... **this works**. I know there may be no results even in two years, but looking at how I’m handling it “well enough” makes me feel that... I will keep going.

Of course, it’s not worth falling into sudden rapture, but I think moments of joy are necessary too — even for such a trivial, small reason. Objectively, it’s still nothing.

Looking ahead is fine, but not to an extreme. I should definitely do fewer self-reckonings like: “Gustaw, instead of improving the blog, procrastinates and thinks about what to do with himself next.”

Luckily, it’s under the watchful care of myself 😉

I know the internet is a dangerous digital jungle. I should verify, and try to find gaps in my knowledge about remote-work security.

Emails can contain malware attachments — so I’ll need to set up a small lab in QEMU on Linux to examine files like that. Given my interests, this is a great source of malware samples. The alias address can stay public. The malware-analysis book I have and the bookmarks I’ve collected over the years may come in handy here. I’m oddly excited about the possibility of analyzing these threats within a controlled environment.❤️Maybe these explorations will bring me some material for a security series?

I’m planning to clean up my host system (by April at the latest). I’ve been using Gentoo as my main and only operating system for a few years now. Unfortunately, I need Windows if I want to be able to work remotely in the future (if that kind of work happens to come my way — I wish I were immortal 💀). I don’t like that operating system, but I’ll have to make a living somehow.

I hate myself for that purchase. For some time now, it’s been hitting my conscience hard — along with my broken ideals. Either way, I want to make a series about the Linux distribution I mentioned and share my experience. In the “operating systems” series, I’ll definitely visit OpenBSD in the future (cybersecurity and crypto topics) and FreeBSD (self-hosting, my own NAS).

I like tinkering with the system.

More and more people are starting to warm up to Linux. The economic situation is also pushing some people to switch:

- People keep their hardware for years and don’t intend to give up equipment that still serves them and still works — it’s enough for them.
- Hardware is getting more expensive, and people are reluctant to replace devices (they probably don’t even know you can live differently when it comes to your digital life).

Hardware prices are rising (people won’t be eager to buy new gear, and corporations will be licking their chops to squeeze you for the “cloud” and for “access” — subscriptions and other crap. “You will own nothing, and you will be happy.”).

I’d really like to have time for everything, but I probably need to cut a few things out of my life.

I’d also like to believe my main plan will work out, but I’d have to deliver at least one solid series in the “what I learned” category. This is my idea for the next stretch of personal work. I need to prepare for it and have my habits developed and ready by then.

Alright. Gustaw, you know what you have to do. Get into the projects and push it.
