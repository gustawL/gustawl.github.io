## What happened?

Today, while going through my emails, I came across a message from Blizzard saying that my Battle.net account has been permanently closed.

In short:  
“Action(s) Taken: Account Closure  
The Battle.net account has violated the Blizzard End User License Agreement. As a result, this Battle.net Account and all associated game accounts have been permanently closed. As the owner of this account, you are responsible for all actions associated with it. We will only overturn penalties if there is evidence of a compromised account.”

Honestly, I don’t know what happened. I submitted a support ticket and I’m hoping for a rational explanation. It was surprisingly hard to even find the right place to open a ticket (from a customer perspective, their website is rough).

## My situation

The account is relatively new. I wasn’t active on the forums, I didn’t write anything stupid to anyone. I played Warcraft 3 occasionally. And yet — a ban and account closure.

For now, I’m waiting for a response, but I’m not optimistic. The only plausible lead that comes to mind is a false alarm (for example because I use a VPN) or some kind of incorrect flag on the account. Of course, these are just hypotheses — until I see specifics from support, I don’t have hard facts.

If a company can cut off access to an account and purchases without a clear justification, it turns into unnecessary stress and chaos.

If this is how they treat paying customers, I need to reconsider what I’m willing to rely on.

## What to do next?

For me, this is a reminder that relying on a single ecosystem is a risk, and that buying games tied to an account-based platform can be a bad deal. Luckily, I’m mentally healthy enough not to get trapped in it — and I don’t own a console. Platforms like PlayStation can deny access to a game you “bought” at any time, and that’s a real risk.

Blizzard is part of a larger chain (Activision → Microsoft), so this situation automatically triggers a question in my head: how much control do I actually have over the things I pay for, and what happens when a platform simply says, “it’s over”?

What should I do after work? Backups, and moving my content creation more toward Google. I don’t want to rely on Microsoft. It feels risky, and Google looks more promising to me. I recently tested NotebookLM and it’s great for what I’m doing — it will be my content tool and part of my workflow. Maybe I should even get a Chromebook, back up what I can, and move on. There’s too much risk with subscriptions and account dependence. What if they suddenly suspend me too?

Sadly, I need to start self-hosting my own stuff. I can’t rely on others.
“The chain is only as strong as its weakest link.”
