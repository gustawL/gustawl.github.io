Privacy is the ability to control how information about you is collected, used, and shared. Bruce Schneier captures this cleanly: “Privacy is about control… A privacy failure is a control failure.” In other words, privacy is not just about “hiding something”; it’s about deciding what flows to whom, when, and for what purpose [^1].

Privacy vs anonymity (the practical breakdown)

- Privacy: your identity can be known, but your data/actions are restricted to the intended audience (or protected from observers). Example: your bank knows who you are, but random third parties shouldn’t be able to see your transactions.
- Anonymity: your actions may be observable, but they aren’t reliably linkable back to you (you’re not identifiable). Example: posting or browsing in a way that doesn’t reveal (or can’t be tied to) your real identity[^2].
