A **knowledge base (KB)** is a _structured collection of knowledge_ (facts, rules, concepts) stored so you can **retrieve** it and, often, **reason** over it.

In **classic** **knowledge** **representation**, KB is a literally modeled as a _set of sentences/formulas_ in some representation language, plus mechanisms to query what follows from them. <br>
>*reference*:<br>[Knowledge representation in logic](https://kcir.pwr.edu.pl/~witold/ai/aie_logic2_s.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That’s what makes it different from “a folder full of stuff”: a KB tries to make knowledge **explicit enough** that you can _ask_ it things (even if “ask” just means search + links + context).

In practice, personal KBs are usually a mix of:
- notes (concepts + explanations),
- snippets (procedures),
- references (sources),
- and links (relationships).

Similar to databases, but still different.
